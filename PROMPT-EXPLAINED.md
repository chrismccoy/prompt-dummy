# What this prompt does: Meta Ad Copy Generator

1. In one line
   Want six ready to test Facebook and Instagram ads for your product? This prompt writes them for you, each with a different way of grabbing attention.

2. What it does
   This prompt turns the AI into an ad writer for paid social media. It is aimed at anyone running ads for a product or service: a small business owner, a marketer, or a freelancer. You tell it what you sell, who you are selling to, the main benefit, and your offer. It hands back six full ad drafts, each written to hook people in a different way, so you can try them out and see which one works best.

3. What you get back
   Six ad drafts, written straight into the chat. Each draft has a first line meant to stop someone scrolling, a main block of ad text, three short headline options, a suggestion for which button to use (like "Shop Now" or "Learn More"), and one line describing the picture or video that would go with it. So a single result might open with a punchy line about a problem your customer has, then a short paragraph, then three headline choices, a button, and a note like "close up video of the product in use".

4. How it works, step by step
   1. You fill in four blanks: what you are selling, who your audience is, the number one result they get, and your offer.
   2. The AI writes six ads, one for each of six set angles: leading with a frustration, painting the dream result, using numbers or reviews, opening a curiosity gap, using a surprising fact, and using honest urgency.
   3. For each ad it writes the hook, the main text, three headlines, a button suggestion, and a visual idea.
   4. It keeps to the size limits: hook up to twelve words, main text fifty to a hundred words, headlines up to six words, one emoji at most.
   5. It follows the ad rules as it writes, so the drafts stay within what Meta allows.

5. The rules it follows
   - Write six ads, one per angle, no more and no less.
   - Keep each part inside its size limit (hook, text, headlines).
   - Use one emoji at most in the main text.
   - Follow Meta ad policy: never mix "you" or "your" with a personal trait like weight, age, money, or health.
   - No fake claims and no fake countdown timers.
   - Write like a real person, not like a faceless brand.

6. What it is good at
   Giving you a spread of ready to test options fast. Because each ad takes a different angle, you get variety in one go instead of six versions of the same idea. The built in rules also keep the drafts inside Meta's policies and away from the pushy, fake sounding tricks that get ads rejected.

7. Where it might trip up
   It only knows what you type into the four blanks. Vague answers give you vague ads. It writes the words but cannot test them, so you still have to run the ads to see what actually sells. It also cannot check your specific account or the latest policy changes, so a final read through before you publish is still on you.

8. How to use it
   Paste the prompt, then fill in the four blanks: what you sell, your target audience, the key benefit, and your offer. Send it and read the six drafts. Pick the angles that fit your product, check the copy against your own facts, and make sure any claim is true before you publish. Then load your favourites into Meta Ads and test them.
